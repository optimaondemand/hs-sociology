❓ THE QUESTION THIS WEEK IS ABOUT
What stands between a person and the state, and does the community you know still have it?
This is the question the whole semester has been circling: Tocqueville named it, Durkheim gave it a mechanism, Tonnies gave it a structure, Nisbet made the political stakes explicit, Putnam supplied the evidence. This week you meet the last of the modern thinkers working out that same diagnosis, and you begin turning the question toward the one place you can actually study closely: the Community Flourishing Capstone, due at the end of Week 18.

💡 THE VIRTUE THIS WEEK IS COURTESY
This week's thinkers agree on the diagnosis and disagree, sometimes sharply, on the remedy. Courtesy here means engaging that disagreement without contempt for the person making it: taking Nisbet's pessimism and Berger and Neuhaus's optimism each on its own merits, rather than picking a side and dismissing the rest. Tuesday's reading gives the same virtue a second, older sense: Tocqueville's account of how equality of conditions extends a shared humanity even across strangers and rivals. Your Capstone's recommendations should be written in that spirit, honest about a community's weaknesses without contempt for the people living inside them.
