🎯 HOW TO CHOOSE YOUR THREE

The Capstone requires at least three primary sources from the table above, integrated into the analysis rather than quoted for decoration. Integration means engaging the actual wording.

  1. One source that supplies a concept applicable to an institution: Durkheim’s social fact (3B), Weber’s authority types (9B), Marx and Engels on class (10B), or Smith on the division of labor (11A).
  2. One source that supplies a method for looking, not only a label: Durkheim’s criteria for a social fact (3B), or Aristotle’s move from household to village to city (1A, 5B). Both transfer to an unfamiliar community.
  3. One source that allows pushback. The strongest Capstones use one thinker to criticize another’s account of the same institution.

Three choices from the same three-week stretch usually read as recent rather than integrative. The rubric rewards synthesis across the term.

✅ THE CAPSTONE CHECKLIST · WEEKS 15 TO 17
  • 1,200 to 1,800 words, formal analytical prose.
  • Opens with a clear description of the community and why it was chosen.
  • Evaluates at least three institutions within that community.
  • Applies at least four frameworks from the course.
  • Engages at least three primary sources printed on the Primary Source Guide pages above, integrated meaningfully into the analysis.
  • Each institution is assessed on both its strengths and its weaknesses.
  • Closes with concrete recommendations, each traceable to a specific sociological claim.
  • Every cited thinker does analytical work in the paragraph where cited.
