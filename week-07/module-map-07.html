Week 7: Education and Tönnies on Community
Start here. This page shows you everything the week contains.
❓ THE QUESTION THIS WEEK IS ABOUT
Is community something you inherit, or something you choose?
Two institutions this week run on opposite versions of that answer. A school assigns you to a classroom of strangers and tries to make something out of the arrangement. A German sociologist named Ferdinand Tönnies spent a career arguing that the modern world was systematically trading inherited bonds for chosen ones, and was not at all sure it was getting the better deal.
YOU ALREADY HAVE THIS DISTINCTION
Week 4: Durkheim's mechanical solidarity (bound by likeness) and organic solidarity (bound by interdependence).
THIS WEEK GIVES IT A SECOND NAME
Tönnies's Gemeinschaft (community) and Gesellschaft (society). Watch for where the two pairings line up, and where they do not.

💡 THE VIRTUE THIS WEEK IS SERVICE
Service here means the habit of acting for a common good you did not personally choose to belong to. Gemeinschaft is where that habit is first learned, inside bonds of family and neighborhood nobody applied for. Tönnies worried that a world built increasingly on Gesellschaft, contracts entered and exited at will, would produce people who never had to practice service because they never had to stay. Watch, this week, for one place in your own life where you act for a group's good simply because you belong to it, not because a contract requires it.
