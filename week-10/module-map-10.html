Week 10: Origins of Government and Political Authority

QUARTER 2 BEGINS
Quarter 1 asked how sociology came to exist and what holds a society together beneath the surface of individual choices. Quarter 2 turns to institutions: government, bureaucracy, economy, and the ordinary structures modern life runs on. It starts with the oldest institution of all, the one every other institution in this quarter depends on for its own authority to mean anything.
❓ THE QUESTION THIS WEEK IS ABOUT
Why do people obey, and what makes some obedience legitimate rather than merely coerced?
Every society that has ever existed has had some form of government. That much is close to a sociological universal. What is not universal is the reason people go along with it. Monday traces where governments and law come from and why force alone is a poor foundation for either. Wednesday gives you Max Weber's answer to the harder question: when people do obey willingly, what kind of claim on them is actually being made?

💡 THE VIRTUE THIS WEEK IS RESPONSIBILITY
Responsibility here means asking what legitimate power owes the people under it, not only what they owe it. Aristotle's whole classification of constitutions turns on whether a government rules for the common good or only for whoever holds office. Weber's typology adds a second question worth sitting with: authority that survives on nothing but personal charisma or naked force is authority that has not yet accepted the discipline of being answerable to a rule. The wrap-up survey will ask where you noticed that discipline, or its absence, this week.
▶ WHERE TO START

Take the Module 10 intro survey first. It asks you to name an authority you obey without ever having examined why, and Thursday's journal will be much easier to write if you have already found a real example rather than reaching for one at the last minute.
