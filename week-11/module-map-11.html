❓ THE QUESTION THIS WEEK IS ABOUT
Two thinkers look at the same industrial world. One finds a process nobody chose. The other finds a structure with beneficiaries. Can both be right?
Monday gives you Weber's account of bureaucracy, rationalization, and the iron cage: an argument with no villain in it. Wednesday gives you the Industrial Revolution and Marx's account of capitalism and alienation: an argument that does name winners and losers. The pairing is the point of the week. You are not being asked to decide which of them is correct.

💡 THE VIRTUE THIS WEEK IS STILL RESPONSIBILITY
Last week, responsibility meant asking what legitimate authority owes the people under it. This week sharpens the question: what do you owe when the system producing an outcome was not designed by anyone in particular? Weber's iron cage has no architect to blame, which makes it tempting to treat it as no one's problem at all. Marx's account insists that someone benefits and that responsibility can still be located even inside a structure. Holding both possibilities open, that an outcome can be nobody's fault and that an outcome can still have beneficiaries who owe something, is the harder and more honest version of the virtue.
