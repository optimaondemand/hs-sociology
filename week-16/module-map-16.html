❓ THE QUESTION THIS WEEK IS ABOUT
How does a society form character once it can no longer assume everyone agrees about the good?
Monday's lesson describes a structural feature of modern moral disagreement. Wednesday's asks a related but separate question: however a society settles, or fails to settle, that disagreement, how does any single person come to have character at all? This week is deliberately taught as sociology, not as an argument for a position, and it asks you to hold two thinkers together who never met: a philosopher writing in Athens and a sociologist writing about French schoolchildren, both asking a version of the same question a modern American sociologist made famous.

💡 THE VIRTUE THIS WEEK IS COURTESY
This week's topic is genuine moral disagreement, and courtesy is what makes it possible to study that disagreement without becoming part of it. A sociologist can describe a conflict precisely, state its strongest objection fairly, and still refuse to tell you which side to take. That refusal is not indecision. It is a discipline, and it is the same discipline that lets two people who disagree all the way down still argue with each other rather than past each other.
