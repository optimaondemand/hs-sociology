⚡ TRY IT · MECHANICAL OR ORGANIC?
Six situations. Decide which kind of solidarity holds each one together.
Tap a situation, then tap a bin. Do not sort by how modern the situation sounds. Sort by what actually does the binding.
The question to ask: are these people bound together because they are alike, or because they need each other to do different things? Durkheim gives a second cue in The Division of Labour I.2 and I.3: look at what happens when someone breaks a rule. Mechanical solidarity punishes to reaffirm who we are. Organic solidarity requires compensation to put things back.

A farming village where nearly everyone does the same work, attends the same church, and deals with a rule-breaker through public shaming.
A hospital where surgeons, nurses, technicians, and cleaners each do work the others cannot do, and none of it functions alone.
A court settles a contract dispute by requiring one company to compensate another for a missed delivery.
A monastery whose members follow an identical rule, wear the same habit, keep the same hours, and pray the same words daily.
A tight immigrant community inside a large city, whose members share a language, a religion, and detailed expectations of one another.
A phone that reaches a buyer only because miners, chemists, engineers, shippers, and retailers who will never meet each did one part.
Mechanical solidarity
Bound by likeness. Strong shared conscience. Punishment reaffirms who we are.
Organic solidarity
Bound by difference. Specialised roles. Law restores what was disrupted.
