Choosing Your Three Sources: A Semester Synthesis and Checklist
There is no second primary source this week. This page exists to help you finish the capstone instead.
📖 WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS

Every other Thursday this term has paired a second thinker with a second reading. This one does not, because there is nothing left to introduce. What you need on the last Thursday of the course is not a new source but a map of the ones you already have, and a way to check your Community Flourishing Capstone against its own requirements before you submit it.

The capstone requires at least three primary source texts from this course, integrated meaningfully rather than as decoration, alongside at least three institutions and at least three classical sociologists. The table below is every primary source this course assigned, in order. Use it to choose three you can genuinely argue with, not just cite.

🎯 HOW TO CHOOSE YOUR THREE

Do not choose by memory alone. Reread the candidates before you commit, because "integrating meaningfully" means engaging the actual wording, not a three-month-old impression of it.

  1. Pick one source that gives you a concept you can apply directly to an institution: Durkheim's solidarity, Weber's authority types, Tönnies's Gemeinschaft and Gesellschaft, or Marx's alienation are the readiest fits.
  2. Pick one source that gives you a method for looking, not just a label: Durkheim's three criteria for a social fact, or Aristotle's move from household to village to city, both transfer well to an unfamiliar community.
  3. Pick one source that lets you push back. The strongest capstones use at least one thinker to criticize another's account of the same institution, and a Pattern A source you can reread in full makes that argument easiest to build with precision.

If your three choices all come from the same three-week stretch, reconsider. A capstone that only draws on Weeks 16 and 17 because they are freshest usually reads as recent rather than integrative. The rubric rewards synthesis across the term, not recency.

✅ BEFORE YOU SUBMIT: THE CAPSTONE CHECKLIST
  • 1,200 to 1,800 words, formal analytical prose.
  • Opens with a clear description of the community and why you chose it.
  • Evaluates at least three institutions within that community.
  • Draws on at least three classical sociologists studied in this course.
  • Engages at least three primary source texts from the course, integrated meaningfully into the analysis, not quoted for decoration.
  • Each institution is assessed on both its strengths and its weaknesses, not one only.
  • Closes with concrete recommendations, and each recommendation is traceable to a specific sociological claim, not to personal opinion alone.
  • No thinker appears only as a name-drop; every one you cite is doing analytical work in the paragraph where you cite them.
