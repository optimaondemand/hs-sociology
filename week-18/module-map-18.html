Week 18: Course Synthesis and Final Examination
The last week. Start here. This page shows you everything it contains.
❓ THE QUESTION THIS WEEK IS ABOUT
Have you developed the sociological imagination this course promised?
Seventeen weeks ago this course opened by naming the moment sociology was born and the man who named it. This week closes the circle: you read Auguste Comte in his own words, you sit a final examination drawn entirely from material you have already mastered, and you finish the capstone that asks whether you can actually use what seventeen thinkers gave you on a real, observed community. There is no new theory this week. There is only the question of whether it took.

There is no Reading Quiz 18. There is nothing new to quiz you on. In its place, the Final Examination draws questions from the seventeen quizzes you already took, in the same proportion across all eighteen weeks. Nobody is writing new questions for it, including your facilitator.

THE TWO QUESTIONS THIS COURSE HAS BEEN ASKING SINCE WEEK 3
Every thinker you read was, in some way, answering one of these.
The solidarity question
What holds a society together, when it works?
The anomie question
What dissolves it, when it fails?

💡 THE VIRTUE THIS WEEK IS PERSEVERANCE
Week 9 asked you to persevere through a quarter's worth of material at its midpoint, when the finish line was not yet visible. This week asks for a different kind of perseverance: finishing well rather than simply finishing. A rushed capstone and a careful one can take the same number of hours. The difference is whether you stayed at the standard through the last paragraph, not just the first one.
▶ WHERE TO START

Take the Module 18 intro survey, then open Lesson 18.01. Look at your capstone draft before Wednesday, not after, so Lesson 18.02's workshop guidance has something to work on.
