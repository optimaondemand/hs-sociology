❓ THE QUESTION THIS WEEK IS ABOUT
What does the system you are formed inside of do to you?
This week's two lessons look modern: mass media, culture industries, globalization. Its two readings are twenty-three centuries apart. Plato worried that a person becomes what he practices imitating, long before anyone had a word for a media environment. Weber describes a discipline that outlived the belief that built it and now shapes people who no longer hold that belief at all. Read together, they are asking the same question in two very different vocabularies: not what a culture teaches, but what it makes of the people raised inside it.

💡 THE VIRTUE THIS WEEK IS STILL SELF-GOVERNMENT
Week 14 asked what a noisy, always-on environment does to attention. This week asks the same question one level up: what does the system that produces the noise, and the system that produces the goods you use, do to the person living inside either one. Plato's answer is that you become what you practice imitating, so choose carefully what you let form you. Weber's answer is that a discipline can outlive the belief that built it and keep shaping people who never held that belief at all, which makes self-government harder, not easier, because the constraint no longer announces itself as a choice anyone is making.
