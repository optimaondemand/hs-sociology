APPENDIX A
Primary Sources Library
Every primary source assigned in this course, in one place, with the one fact that matters most: where you actually find the words.

Most weeks, both readings sit right on the primary source guide page, reproduced in full because the translation is in the public domain. A smaller number of weeks assign a reading that is still under copyright in English, even though the original German, French, or Latin work is not. For those, the guide names the exact edition, tells you precisely where to start and stop, and quotes a few short lines with commentary, but the book itself is something you obtain separately.

That difference matters for planning. If your week's reading is a library book, you cannot start it the night before. Check the table below at the start of the quarter and place any holds or interlibrary loans early.

"On this page" means the excerpt is printed in full on that week's primary source guide, at no cost to you. ⚠️ ABOUT THE LIBRARY-BOOK READINGS ABOVE
Students must obtain lawful access to the assigned literary work separately. Optima course materials are original instructional materials designed to support analysis, discussion, and assessment of the work. Copyrighted text, images, illustrations, panels, audio, or other protected materials are not reproduced in Canvas, VR, videos, downloadable files, or teacher resources except where expressly licensed, in the public domain, or approved as a limited fair-use excerpt for instructional analysis.
▶ IF YOUR WEEK NEEDS A LIBRARY BOOK

Every primary source guide that assigns a library book gives you the exact edition, including the translator, plus a table for getting the book: your school library first, then your public library, then a bookshop or publisher. Page numbers are never the only locator, because they change between printings; each guide instead gives you a structural location, book and chapter, plus the first and last words of the passage, so you can confirm you are in the right place without needing the same printing as anyone else.

If you cannot reach a copy in time, tell your facilitator before the reading is due, not after.
