APPENDIX E
Major Assignments Overview
This course has four major projects, thirty percent of your grade, spaced across the semester so each builds on the last. This page is the map of all four. Each project's own assignment page carries the full requirements and rubric.
THE FOUR PROJECTS ACROSS THE SEMESTER
WEEKS 1–8
Foundations. Work toward the Thinker Profile.
WEEKS 9–11
Community Institution Study.
WEEKS 12–15
Sociologist Case Study Reports.
WEEKS 16–18
Community Flourishing Capstone.
1Classical Thinker Profile
Built across Weeks 1–8 400–600 words Due end of Week 8

An intellectual portrait, not a biography, of one classical sociologist studied in Quarter 1. You explain the problem the thinker was trying to solve, the concepts they developed to solve it, and why those concepts still matter.

  • Engages at least one primary source by the thinker you choose.
  • Covers the thinker's central problem, their key concepts in your own words, one specific contemporary application, and one honest limitation of their framework.
2Community Institution Study
Weeks 9–11 700–1,000 words Due end of Week 11

A field observation report on a real public institution: a church, a voluntary association, a neighborhood organization, a civic meeting, a school event, or a local government body. Your evidence is what you directly observed, not what you read about institutions in general.

  • Requires at least one observation session of at least 45 minutes, with detailed notes taken on site.
  • Applies at least four sociological concepts from the course to what you observed.
  • Engages at least two primary sources.

If you see a different due date referenced anywhere else for this project, the date on this page and in the syllabus, end of Week 11, is the correct one.

3Sociologist Case Study Reports
Weeks 12–15 4 × 350–500 words Due end of Week 15

Four short reports, submitted as four separate files, each applying one thinker's concept to one specific historical or contemporary case: Weber, Marx, Durkheim, and one community theorist of your choice.

  • Each report applies exactly one concept from that thinker to a specific case, not a general survey of the thinker's work.
  • Each report engages at least one primary source by that report's thinker.
  • Each report names one thing the concept fails to explain about the case.
4Community Flourishing Capstone
Weeks 16–18 1,200–1,800 words Due end of Week 18

The course's summative paper. You evaluate a community you have studied, observed, or belong to, and argue what its condition reveals about what community flourishing requires.

  • Evaluates at least three institutions of your chosen community.
  • Applies at least three classical sociologists' frameworks.
  • Engages at least three primary sources.
  • Ends with specific recommendations, and addresses the strongest objection to your own argument.
📋 WHAT ALL FOUR PROJECTS HAVE IN COMMON

Every project is a native Canvas Assignment with its own attached rubric, and every rubric scores the same four things: understanding of the material, quality of reasoning, clarity and precision of expression, and intellectual honesty about a framework's limits. The specific point values differ by project, and each project's own assignment page carries its full rubric.

Every project also follows the file naming and submission process in Appendix B: save your draft under your own name, keep every draft, and submit the file labeled _Final through Canvas.
