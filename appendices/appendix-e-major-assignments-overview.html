APPENDIX E
Major Assignments Overview
Four major projects, thirty percent of the grade, each building on the last. Each project's assignment page carries the full requirements and rubric.
THE FOUR PROJECTS ACROSS THE SEMESTER
WEEKS 1–8
Foundations. Work toward the Thinker Profile.
WEEKS 9–10
Community Institution Study.
WEEKS 11–14
Sociologist Case Study Reports.
WEEKS 15–17
Community Flourishing Capstone.
1Classical Thinker Profile
Built across Weeks 1–8 400–600 words Due end of Week 8

An intellectual portrait, not a biography, of one classical sociologist studied in Quarter 1: the problem the thinker was trying to solve, the concepts developed to solve it, and why those concepts still matter.

  • Engages at least one primary source by the thinker you choose.
  • Covers the thinker's central problem, their key concepts in your own words, one specific contemporary application, and one honest limitation of their framework.
2Community Institution Study
Weeks 9–10 700–1,000 words Due end of Week 10

A field observation report on a real public institution: a church, a voluntary association, a neighborhood organization, a civic meeting, a school event, or a local government body. The evidence is what you directly observed.

  • Requires at least one observation session of at least 45 minutes, with detailed notes taken on site.
  • Applies at least four sociological concepts from the course to what you observed.
  • Engages at least two primary sources.
3Sociologist Case Study Reports
Weeks 11–14 4 × 350–500 words Due end of Week 14

Four short reports in one Canvas submission, each applying one thinker's concept to one specific historical case: Weber, Marx, Durkheim, and one community theorist of your choice (Tönnies, Nisbet, or Putnam).

  • Each report applies exactly one concept from that thinker to a different case, not a survey of the thinker's work.
  • Each report engages at least one primary source by that report's thinker.
  • Each report names one thing the concept fails to explain about the case.
4Community Flourishing Capstone
Weeks 15–17 1,200–1,800 words Due end of Week 17

The course's summative paper: an evaluation of a community you have studied, observed, or belong to, arguing what its condition reveals about what community flourishing requires.

  • Evaluates at least three institutions of your chosen community.
  • Applies at least four frameworks from the course.
  • Engages at least three primary sources.
  • Ends with specific recommendations, and addresses the strongest objection to your own argument.
📋 WHAT ALL FOUR PROJECTS HAVE IN COMMON

Every project is a native Canvas Assignment with an attached rubric. Point values differ by project; each assignment page carries its full rubric.

Primary sources. A primary source is a text printed on a Primary Source Guide page: the thinker’s own words, in the public domain. Text Study pages and lesson pages are course material on a thinker; they inform the work but do not count toward a project’s primary source requirement. Exception: Simmel and Tönnies. Neither has a primary-source reading in the course. For these two thinkers only, a Text Study page counts as the primary source (Simmel: 4B, 5A, 13A, 13B; Tönnies: 7B).

Every project also follows the file naming and submission process in Appendix B: save every draft under your own name and submit the file labeled _Final through Canvas.
