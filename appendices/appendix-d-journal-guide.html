APPENDIX D
Sociological Journal Guide
Weeks 1 to 8: written entries. Weeks 9 to 17: video discussions.
📖 WHAT THE JOURNAL IS FOR

The journal trains one habit: applying a theoretical framework to something actually observed, and moving between the personal and the structural, the specific and the general. It is not a diary and not a summary of the week’s lesson.

Read together, the seventeen entries record how a sociological vision develops across the course. The Capstone draws on them.

TWO FORMATS
WEEKS 1 TO 8
Written entry
200 to 350 words, typed into the Canvas item.
  • Odd weeks (1, 3, 5, 7): graded Discussion. Reply to one classmate by the end of the following week.
  • Even weeks (2, 4, 6, 8): Assignment scored on completion. No reply.
WEEKS 9 TO 17
Video discussion
Graded Discussion every week, 20 points.
  • Post: one video, 2 to 4 minutes.
  • Reply: one classmate, by the end of the week.

Each week’s Module Map names the format.

✏️ FIVE MOVES, IN WRITING OR ON VIDEO
  1. Name a concept. One or more specific concepts from the week, with the thinker who developed it.
  2. Identify a situation. Something specific from your own observation: one platform, one interaction, one moment, not “social media in general.”
  3. Apply the concept. What it reveals about the situation that would not be visible without it.
  4. Complicate it. What the concept does not explain, or where it falls short.
  5. Conclude. What changed in your understanding of the situation, the concept, or your own social experience.
🎥 VIDEO DISCUSSIONS, WEEKS 9 TO 17
PartRequirement
Video post2 to 4 minutes, one take, no slides. In the discussion reply box: Insert, Media, Record/Upload Media. The prompt on the discussion page gives the question and the parts the video covers.
Peer replyOne classmate’s video, answered in video or text. The reply makes one specific move listed on the discussion page: it tests a criterion, asks a question that complicates the analysis, or offers another framework. Agreement or praise alone does not count.
DuePost and reply, end of the week.

Full credit, 20 points:

  • Video of 2 to 4 minutes, posted by the deadline.
  • A specific, real situation, not a general impression.
  • The week’s concept named and applied with concrete evidence.
  • One reply that makes a move named in the prompt.
📋 WRITTEN ENTRIES, WEEKS 1 TO 8

200 to 350 words in full sentences, entered directly in the Canvas Discussion or Assignment, not as a separate document.

Odd weeks (Discussion, three-point scale, plus one point for the peer reply):

PointsWhat earns it
3 (full credit)Submitted by the deadline, meets the word count, names and correctly applies at least one specific concept, and engages a specific situation with real analytical attention.
2 (partial credit)Submitted, but thin, vague, or not applying a concept from the course.
0Not submitted by the deadline, or off-topic.
+1 (peer reply)The reply to one classmate’s entry does at least one of the following: identifies a concept the classmate applied well and says why it works; offers an alternative framework for the same situation; raises a specific question that complicates the analysis. Agreement or a compliment alone does not earn the point.

Even weeks (Assignment, completion-scored, no peer reply):

ResultWhat earns it
CompleteSubmitted by the deadline, meets the word count, and names and applies at least one specific concept to a specific situation. Genuine engagement is the bar, not polish.
IncompleteNot submitted by the deadline, well under the word count, or no specific concept.
💡 THE JOURNAL AS A COURSE-LONG RECORD
The Week 17 video returns to the Week 1 entry. Seventeen entries form one record of intellectual development.
