❓ THE QUESTION THIS WEEK IS ABOUT
What does a society need to stay orderly, and how much pressure to conform is too much?
Monday and Tuesday ask whether a society could ever eliminate crime, and why Durkheim thought the answer was no, and thought that mattered. Wednesday and Thursday turn the same question around: Tocqueville is worried not that America tolerates too little deviance, but that it may tolerate too little dissent. Both halves of the week are about where a society draws its lines, and what happens at the edges.

💡 THE VIRTUE THIS WEEK IS COURAGE
Courage here means facing an uncomfortable social fact without flinching or explaining it away. Durkheim insists that crime cannot be wished out of existence and asks you to sit with that rather than reach for a tidier answer. Tocqueville insists that a free society can quietly punish honest disagreement without ever passing a law against it, and asks you to notice the pressure rather than assume that no law means no coercion. Both arguments are uncomfortable on purpose.
